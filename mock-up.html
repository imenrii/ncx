(2) Contours — Met Office style

Met Office surface analyses draw isobars as one uniform thin black line at 4 hPa. No line is emphasised: the pattern carries the meaning, and a thick line at every 8 hPa invents a hierarchy the data does not have. The value is written in a break in the line, not on top of it, and the break is what keeps the figure readable.

Now. 1.75 / 1.5 / 1.0 px by level, white halo 1 px under every line, label with a 3 px white stroke halo. The halo ring reads as a second, pale contour.

Proposed. One 1.15 px black line at every level, L and H at the centres, and every label in a masked break. Interval —.

Proposed, zoomed. Same line weight. A zoom spreads the lines, so the interval returns to 4 hPa and labels repeat along each line. Interval —.

Interval follows the pane, not the source

Every level is labelled, so the level set itself has to stay readable. The base is 4 hPa. If the median gap between neighbouring isobars falls under 13 px, the interval doubles: 4 → 8 → 16 → 32 hPa. The same field in a narrow pane therefore shows fewer lines, not thinner ones.

(2) Vectors — density, size, style

Arrow spacing must be a screen quantity, not a data quantity: the same chart at 300 px and at 1200 px needs the same arrow spacing in millimetres, or it reads as noise at one size and as emptiness at the other.

Length-scaled arrows lose the light-wind field and overshoot in a jet. Barbs give speed at the cost of a much larger mark, so they need about 1.6 × the spacing. The uniform arrow is the default because the colour map already carries magnitude.

(2.1) Both overlays, over a colour map

Three layers compete for the same pixels. The proposal keeps them on separate visual channels: the map owns colour, the contours own continuous thin line, the wind owns short repeated mark.

Proposal. Uniform black isobars with masked labels, 1.3 px arrows with a light casing, and the legend in the corner.

Why it holds on a dark map

  • Black keeps the highest contrast against viridis and thermal; both are dark only at one end.
  • The contour needs no casing at 1.15 px: the mask break, not a halo, is what makes the label readable.
  • The arrow is short and repeated, so a thin white casing at 2.2 px is enough on the dark end and is nearly invisible on the light end.
  • On greys, black overlays lose. Keep this map for fields without overlays, or dim the map to 0–70 % luminance while an overlay is on.
  • The legend corner is reserved: arrows and isobar labels are rejected inside it. A white casing alone fails when an arrow lands across the word.

Order

  1. colour field
  2. coastlines
  3. isobars
  4. isobar labels

Labels above lines, arrows above labels: a label is a hole in the line, and an arrow crossing a hole still reads.

The analog drawing rules, applied

Meteorology for Mariners gives six rules for drawing isobars by hand. A contour algorithm satisfies most of them by construction. Two changed the rendering.

RuleStatus here
1. Loose ends at the frame, or closed curvesAutomatic. A traced line either reaches the frame or closes on itself. Sharp angles at fronts cannot appear: this view has no front analysis.
2. Never cross, touch or joinAutomatic. One level per cell edge. But the label breaks are holes, and in a tight gradient a reader can join a break to the neighbouring line. Label boxes already reject overlap; they should also reject a position where the neighbouring isobar is nearer than the break is long.
3. High on one side all alongAutomatic, from the sign of the cell crossing.
4. Constant interval, the col exceptedEnforced: every level is a multiple of the chosen interval. At a col the same level appears twice, which the tracer draws as two separate lines. Correct.
5. As smooth as the observations allowChanged. Marching squares gives a line with a corner at every cell. Two rounds of corner cutting now run before drawing. Corner cutting stays inside the original polygon, so it cannot push a line across its neighbour and break rule 2.
6. Buys Ballot: wind along the isobar, slightly toward lowA check, not a rule to draw by. The data decides. But it is the one cheap test that the two overlays agree: arrows nearly parallel to the isobars with a small inward angle is right; arrows across the isobars means a swapped u/v pair, a sign error, or a southern-hemisphere field. Worth a warning, not a correction.

Decisions taken

  • L / H centre marks are drawn, with the central value and a masked break in the lines behind them.
  • Every level is labelled. Pile-up is controlled by the interval, which doubles from 4 hPa while the median gap is under 13 px.
  • The interval note lives in the metadata panel, not on the plot.
  • The legend marks share a 36 px column, so Pressure and Wind vector start on the same x. The pressure mark is 36 px against a 62 px word: 0.58.

Still open

  • A centre needs a minimum depth to be worth a mark. A flat field produces weak extrema; the mock-up marks every local extremum it finds.
  • Fronts are the other half of a synoptic chart. They are not derivable from one pressure field, so they stay out.
